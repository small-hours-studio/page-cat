How it moves

Body and legs

A deep chest, a tucked waist and a round rump. The thigh and upper arm sit inside the body, so only the lower legs show, the way they do on a real cat.

Gaits change with speed

Walking follows a cat's real footfall order (left hind, left front, right hind, right front). Faster, it trots in diagonal pairs. Flat out, it gallops: hind paws land, then front, the spine bunches and stretches, and all four paws leave the ground.

Jumps

It wiggles, aims at the spot, and launches. The body tilts along the arc, the head leads, the front paws reach for the edge, and the front lands first.

Cursor play

Mostly the cat does its own thing. Once in a while a resting cursor catches its eye: it stalks low, wiggles and pounces, or, if the cursor is at head height, walks over and rubs its head on it. Then it ignores you for a good while.

Page play

Now and then it swats a small icon or a short word off the page and hunts it like a mouse. The item darts, freezes, jinks, and hides behind the cards at the bottom, peeking out until the cat paws it loose. It tires as it runs, so long chases end in a catch. Then the item floats back to its exact spot.

Scratching

It rears up against the side of the window or a card and pulls down slowly with alternating paws, or rakes along a flat surface. Claw marks are drawn on the cat's own layer only; the page is untouched.

Coats and patterns

Six coat colors and five patterns: solid, tabby (stripes, tail rings, forehead "M"), tuxedo (white bib, belly, muzzle and socks), calico (white with orange and black patches; it ignores the coat color) and colorpoint (darker face, ears, legs and tail). Patterns are painted only onto the cat, so they move with every pose.

Activity

Chill cats mostly sit, groom and loaf. Active cats get the zoomies (darting in random directions, leaping onto things, pouncing on nothing, grabbing toys mid-run) and hunt page items and your cursor far more often.
